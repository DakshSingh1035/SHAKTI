SHAKTI SECURITY, PRIVACY, AND RESPONSIBLE AI
=============================================

This document states design requirements for a future implementation. It is
not a certification or legal opinion.

1. SECURITY CONTROLS
--------------------

* Give every device a unique identity and per-device cryptographic credentials.
* Encrypt app, band, backend, camera, and operator communication.
* Use authenticated BLE pairing and reject unauthenticated control commands.
* Store keys securely where the hardware supports it.
* Never hardcode production secrets in firmware or application packages.
* Use role-based access control and multi-factor authentication for operators.
* Audit sensitive reads, exports, configuration changes, and escalations.
* Rate-limit ingestion without blocking genuine emergency events.
* Use secure boot, signed firmware updates, and rollback protection where
  supported.
* Maintain vulnerability reporting, patching, and incident response processes.

2. PRIVACY RULES
----------------

* Collect the minimum location, media, and camera data required for the stated
  safety function.
* Obtain explicit, understandable consent for location sharing and recording.
* Define retention periods before collecting data.
* Restrict camera access to authorized people and documented purposes.
* Avoid facial recognition and identity inference by default.
* Keep safety alerts separate from surveillance or employee monitoring.
* Provide access, correction, deletion, and grievance processes where required.
* Explain degraded service and uncertain location instead of hiding limitations.

3. RESPONSIBLE AI
-----------------

AI output is a candidate signal. It is not proof of a crime, a person's
identity, intent, or guilt. Models must be evaluated using representative
benign and concerning examples, with precision, recall, false-alert rate,
missed-event rate, latency, calibration, and subgroup/coverage results.

Human review is required before consequential camera-based escalation. Review
outcomes, false positives, and missed events should be recorded to improve the
system without turning the model into an autonomous authority.

4. EVIDENCE INTEGRITY
---------------------

Evidence handling should record incident ID, device identity, capture times,
clock source, location quality, upload time, software version, media digest,
and access history. SHA-256 protects against unnoticed changes after hashing,
but provenance also requires secure devices, trusted time, access control,
consent, and appropriate legal process.

5. REGULATORY DEPENDENCIES
--------------------------

Before deployment in India, obtain specialist review for data protection,
CCTV access, surveillance, telecom and spectrum requirements, satellite
equipment, recording consent, emergency-service agreements, and police data
sharing. Formal authorization is required before connecting to agency systems
or using camera feeds in live operations.

6. SECURITY TESTING PLAN
-----------------------

Future testing should include:

* Threat modelling of the app, band, APIs, operator tools, and evidence store.
* Device identity, secure pairing, firmware update, and key-storage testing.
* Offline queue recovery, duplicate submission, and replay testing.
* Authorization tests for every incident, evidence, and camera operation.
* Rate-limit, abuse, and denial-of-service resilience testing.
* Audit-log integrity and retention/deletion policy tests.
* Privacy review of location, media, and camera data flows.
* Human factors testing for accidental SOS and misleading status language.
