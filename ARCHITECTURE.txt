SHAKTI ARCHITECTURE NOTE
========================

PURPOSE
-------
This document describes the proposed architecture for Shakti, a unified
women's-safety platform. It is a design reference only; it is not an
implementation specification for a completed system.

1. ARCHITECTURAL GOALS
----------------------

The design prioritizes:

* Fast and difficult-to-trigger-accidentally manual SOS activation.
* Useful local behaviour during internet or cellular outages.
* Multiple independent communication paths where hardware and service permit.
* Explicit delivery and acknowledgement states.
* Minimal, consent-based data collection.
* Human review for camera-generated candidate events.
* Traceable evidence and administrative actions.
* Replaceable components and staged validation.

2. LOGICAL LAYERS
-----------------

DEVICE LAYER
The wristband contains the SOS button, haptic motor, low-power controller,
BLE radio, battery subsystem, and optional LTE/GNSS or satellite modules. The
device can create and retain an incident without the phone or internet.

MOBILE LAYER
The Android application pairs with the wristband, manages the account and
contacts, obtains phone location, sends notifications, displays precise
delivery status, and provides user consent controls.

BACKEND LAYER
The backend authenticates devices and users, accepts incident events, stores
the incident lifecycle, coordinates notification attempts, receives
acknowledgements, correlates authorized camera events, and writes audit data.

AI LAYER
AI services are isolated from the core incident workflow. Personal-safety
models may analyze opted-in signals. CCTV models begin with person detection
and anonymous tracking on authorized recorded footage. AI produces candidates
and confidence values, not final criminal determinations.

PUBLIC-SAFETY LAYER
Authorized cameras, monitoring operators, emergency contacts, and approved
agency procedures connect through controlled interfaces. Consequential action
requires policy, authorization, and human review.

EVIDENCE LAYER
Permitted media and metadata are encrypted, stored privately, hashed, and
accessed through role-based controls. Every review, export, or deletion is
audited and subject to retention rules.

3. INCIDENT LIFECYCLE
---------------------

An incident should use a globally unique incident ID and a monotonically
increasing device sequence number.

BOOT_SELF_TEST
  Check button, memory, battery, radio interfaces, and firmware integrity.

NORMAL
  Low-power monitoring, BLE availability, and routine status reporting.

SOS_TRIGGERED
  The button event has passed validation and the local incident is committed.

LOCATION_ACQUISITION
  Acquire GNSS or use a clearly labelled phone/last-known location with
  accuracy, source, timestamp, and age.

BLE_DELIVERY / LTE_DELIVERY / SATELLITE_DELIVERY
  Attempt configured transport paths. Paths may run in parallel or in a
  configured sequence.

OFFLINE_QUEUED
  No receiver has acknowledged delivery. Keep the event in durable storage,
  retry with backoff, and preserve the emergency queue slot.

SERVER_RECEIVED
  The backend has authenticated and accepted the incident.

RECIPIENT_ACKNOWLEDGED
  A person or authorized service has acknowledged the incident. This is
  intentionally separate from SERVER_RECEIVED.

CLOSED / CANCELLED
  The lifecycle has completed with an audit record and an appropriate reason.

4. SOS DATA FLOW
----------------

1. The wearer presses and holds the band button or activates SOS in the app.
2. The device validates the action and gives local haptic feedback.
3. The device persists the incident before transmission.
4. The system starts location acquisition and records location quality.
5. BLE transfers the event to the paired phone when available.
6. Independent LTE may send directly from the wristband when configured.
7. Optional satellite or local relay paths may be attempted when supported.
8. The backend deduplicates by incident ID and returns an authenticated receipt.
9. Notification services contact permitted recipients.
10. Recipient acknowledgement and escalation are recorded separately.
11. Unacknowledged or undelivered incidents remain pending and retry.

5. CAMERA EVENT FLOW
--------------------

1. An authorized analytics service detects a candidate event.
2. The backend validates camera authorization, location, time, confidence,
   and coverage metadata.
3. The system checks opted-in users inside the configured warning radius.
4. Relevant users receive a limited warning through the app; paired bands may
   vibrate over BLE.
5. A control-room operator reviews the candidate before consequential action.
6. Review outcomes, false positives, evidence access, and escalation are
   written to the audit trail.

6. RELIABILITY RULES
--------------------

* Persist first, transmit second.
* Make retries idempotent.
* Never overwrite an unresolved SOS with routine telemetry.
* Reserve queue capacity for emergency incidents.
* Apply retry backoff and define retention/expiry rules explicitly.
* Display pending, server received, recipient acknowledged, and closed as
  different states.
* Report stale or uncertain locations honestly.
* Never represent a configured fallback as guaranteed coverage.

7. DEPLOYMENT BOUNDARIES
------------------------

The architecture assumes that camera access, emergency-service APIs, spectrum,
satellite service, and telecom features require external authorization and
validation. These dependencies must be represented as explicit deployment
contracts rather than hidden assumptions in the application.
